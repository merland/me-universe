# megabit.se

The domain of my company [megabit-rd-ab](megabit-rd-ab.md) (2026-10-10, me). It is registered with AWS Route 53, and its DNS is hosted there too (2026-10-09, me). Email for the domain is handled by Google Workspace (2026-10-09, me). A catch-all, configured in Google Workspace routing, delivers mail to every address on the domain to my main Google Workspace account, martin@erlandsson.se, described on [erlandsson-se](erlandsson-se.md) (2026-10-09, me).

The domain is mainly for email. It also has a placeholder website that contains only a simple contact form (2026-10-09, me). The site is hosted on AWS Lightsail as a simple container deployed by AWS; form submissions end up in my "My forms" base in Airtable, see [services](services.md) (2026-10-10, me).

## How it sits in Google Workspace

megabit.se is a user alias domain for erlandsson.se in the Admin console, not a secondary domain: every address at erlandsson.se automatically exists at megabit.se too, so martin@megabit.se resolves to my single user (2026-10-20, me, Admin console). martin@megabit.se is also a send-as identity in that account (2026-10-10, journal/2026-10-10). The MX record is Google's single `1 smtp.google.com`, replaced on 2026-10-20 from the five legacy records, after which the Admin console shows the domain's email setup as All OK; details on [email-authentication](email-authentication.md) (2026-10-20, session).

What this gives in Calendar and Drive, confirmed by test from my old Gmail account: a calendar invite and a Drive share sent to martin@megabit.se land in the erlandsson.se calendar and Drive (2026-10-20, me). What it cannot give: invites, sharing invites and document ownership always show the user's primary address, martin@erlandsson.se; an alias is never the visible identity (2026-10-20, session, Google admin help). The primary address cannot be flipped to martin@megabit.se while the domain is an alias domain; that would need removing it and re-adding it as a secondary domain, with a bounce window and the DKIM key redone (2026-10-20, session). A separate megabit.se identity therefore needs a second Workspace seat; a personal Google account at the address is blocked as a conflicting account, and Cloud Identity Free has no Calendar (2026-10-20, session, Google docs). Business Standard includes shared drives, so company files can live in a shared drive owned by the organisation rather than by my user; none created yet (2026-10-20, session).

Outgoing mail is authenticated with SPF, DKIM and DMARC at policy `p=quarantine` since 2026-10-20, verified end to end on 2026-10-10 and accepted by Kivra; details are on [email-authentication](email-authentication.md) (2026-10-10, me).
