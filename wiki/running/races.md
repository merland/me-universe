## Varvetmilen 10K, April 2026

Watch distance 10.047 km, average pace about 5:24/km, average HR 184, session max 199, raw one-second peak 200 bpm confirmed by RR intervals; cadence about 182 steps/min; roughly flat course; recorded temperature about 17 C (2026-10-09, source).

Pace was remarkably even, the ten kilometre splits varying by only about 4 seconds, while HR climbed steadily from 163 in km 1 to 194 in km 10. First half averaged about 177 bpm, second half about 190, with pace only 9 seconds slower over the second 5 km and step length unchanged (2026-10-09, source). No mechanical collapse over 54 minutes.

The file carried Suunto ZoneSense data, but its baseline started while I was already racing, so its zone totals are not trustworthy (2026-10-09, source).

## Göteborgsvarvet half marathon, May 2026

Watch distance 21.407 km, about 1.5 percent long, so watch paces are slightly optimistic; average HR 180, session max 199, raw peak 200; cadence about 182 steps/min; ascent about 127 m; recorded temperature from about 22 C early to 27–28 C late (2026-10-09, source).

Pace by 5 km block: 5:25, 5:18, 5:38, 5:58 per km; HR 171, 180, 181, 184; cadence held at 183 until the last block, where it fell to 178; step length fell from about 1.03 m to 0.94 m (2026-10-09, source). The second 10 km was about 8 percent slower than the first with less climbing, so terrain does not explain it. Three brief very-slow episodes came at about 17.1, 19.3 and 20.1 km. Around km 20 pace fell to about 6:24/km, yet I re-accelerated to about 5:57/km for km 21 and about 4:51/km for the last 400 m at HR up to 199 (2026-10-09, source).

What it felt like: very good in the first half; from halfway the whole lower body hurt, mostly the thighs, worsening until the last kilometres were the most painful race I have run. I finished because I was committed to sub two hours. Afterwards I developed an overuse injury in the lower back and hip that took months to heal (2026-10-09, source, me). I wore Merrell VaporGlove.

A 54-minute 10K predicts about a 1:59–2:00 half marathon, so the time was not disproportionately slow; I likely reached the predicted time by pushing through, not because durability was adequate (2026-10-09, source).

## Strava race predictions

Strava's half-marathon prediction was 2:02:43 (5:49/km) in the week before the race, jumped to 1:53:34 (5:23/km, almost my 10K pace) in the week after it, then drifted back toward 2:00–2:02 over the following months (2026-10-09, source, from my screenshots). The jump suggests the race data looked stronger to Strava's model than the finish time; the later drift plausibly reflects reduced training during the injury, which is inference (2026-10-09, source).
