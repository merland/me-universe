# Half-marathon race plan by heart rate

A race-execution model for the fastest possible half marathon, from the continuation AI session filed as [sources/2026-10-19-running-physiology-ai-session-summary-2.md](../../sources/2026-10-19-running-physiology-ai-session-summary-2.md); "source" below means that file. It is built on my working estimates of max HR 203, LT1 171 and LT2 190 from [running/physiology](physiology.md), and on an explicit hypothetical: that I have raised tissue durability enough for the cardiovascular engine, not the legs, to be the limiter (2026-10-19, source). It aims at performance, not conservative completion (2026-10-19, source). It is a model from a session; I have neither raced it nor decided to (2026-10-19, me).

## The shape of the race

Pace nearly flat, HR gradually rising, then both pace and HR rising at the end (2026-10-19, source). The first 1–3 km go at goal pace or 2–5 s/km slower while HR comes up; km 4–15 hold a very steady goal pace while HR drifts up; km 16–18 stay near goal pace as HR approaches LT2; from km 19 pace is held or raised while HR may pass LT2 (2026-10-19, source). HR rises at constant pace because of early HR lag, cardiovascular drift, rising body temperature and fatigue, so a rising HR is no reason to slow while pace is sustainable, and a low early HR is no reason to speed up: HR lags demand in the first kilometres, which makes the early targets ceilings rather than values to chase (2026-10-19, source). A slight negative split is fine; a deliberate large split in either direction is not (2026-10-19, source). An illustration at a 5:00/km goal: splits of about 5:03, 5:01, 5:00, 4:59, 5:01 while HR runs 170, 177, 181, 183, 185 and on upward (2026-10-19, source, illustration only).

## HR targets

Working tolerance about ±2–3 bpm around each band; the average over such a race would be about 183–186 bpm (2026-10-19, source).

| Phase | Target HR |
|---|---|
| km 0–5 | about 175–181 |
| km 5–15 | about 181–187 |
| km 15–19 | about 187–190 |
| km 19–21.1 | 190 and up toward a maximal finish |

Rule of thumb: under about 185 through halfway, 185–190 through the second half, no deliberate crossing of 190 until roughly the last 3 km (2026-10-19, source).

## Why not sit at LT2 from the start

190 bpm is about 94 percent of my max. It can be held for a long time, but racing at or above it early builds metabolic strain that cannot be sustained, and HR drifts upward anyway at constant pace. So most of the race stays below LT2, approaches it late, and crosses it deliberately only in the final kilometres, where HR stops being useful as a limiter and the race is run on whatever is left (2026-10-19, source).

## Reading the numbers on the day

The table is a sanity check and guardrail, not a value to hit every kilometre: about 190 at km 6 is too hard barring heat or sensor trouble; about 180 at km 15 while feeling very comfortable may mean room to push; 188–190 at km 17 while holding pace is normal (2026-10-19, source). Heat, humidity, dehydration, hills, wind, illness, fatigue, caffeine, sleep and race-day adrenaline all shift HR; on a hot day sustainable HR for a given pace may be several beats higher while sustainable pace is lower, and hills make per-kilometre targets noisy (2026-10-19, source). Effort is judged from HR, pace, perceived effort, terrain and conditions together (2026-10-19, source).

## What success would look like

In the 2026 Göteborgsvarvet, on [running/races](races.md), HR rose while pace, cadence and step length fell and leg pain took over. A successful race on this model would show pace almost flat, HR rising steadily, mechanics stable, and a finish where HR passes LT2 while pace holds or rises. That signature is what would show the engine, not the chassis, had become the limiter (2026-10-19, source).
