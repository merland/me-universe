What the run shows (2026-11-03, session):

- HR took about 5 minutes to reach 140 and settled at 145–152 from km 2 onward, so the whole run sat in the easy band of [running/physiology](physiology.md), 20 bpm or more below the working LT1 of 171; no second spent at or above 160 bpm.
- Cardiac drift was small: over km 2–10 the second half ran at the same pace as the first with HR 5 bpm higher, a speed-to-HR decoupling of about 2.5 percent, within the 5 percent usually taken as an aerobic effort held comfortably.
- Cadence and step length held steady for the whole hour apart from the climb, so nothing in this file suggests the form breakdown seen late in the May 2026 half marathon.
- The watch's own estimates, not measurements: estimated VO2max 46.6 ml/kg/min, training effect 2.6, peak EPOC 45, TSS 55.
- Suunto ZoneSense: the first ten minutes were genuinely easy, so the baseline is plausibly valid this time; DDFA values began after about 18 minutes and stayed mostly negative, meaning aerobic. The file states an aerobic threshold of 149.5 bpm and counts 6.5 minutes in its anaerobic zone. That threshold is 20 bpm below the working LT1 on [running/physiology](physiology.md); whether that is ZoneSense's definition, its calibration on a first easy run, or a real change is open (2026-11-03, session, unconfirmed).
- The RR series has artefacts, with intervals from 0.19 s to 59 s, so it should be cleaned before any HRV use; the record HR, from the belt, is clean.
- The watch's HR zones are still the factory defaults: it counted the whole run as zone 1 or 2 (2026-11-03, sources/2026-11-03-morning-run-10k.fit).
